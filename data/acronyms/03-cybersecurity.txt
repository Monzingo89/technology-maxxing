identity|IAM|Identity and Access Management|Manages identities and their permitted access.|Identify the person, then Allow the right Material.|Authentication and authorization are separate decisions.
identity|AAA|Authentication, Authorization, and Accounting|Verifies identity, decides permissions, and records activity.|Who are you? What can you do? What did you do?|Accounting means activity records here, not financial bookkeeping.
identity|MFA|Multi-Factor Authentication|Requires evidence from more than one authentication-factor category.|Many Factors Authenticate.|Two passwords are two secrets, not two different factors.
identity|2FA|Two-Factor Authentication|Requires two distinct authentication-factor categories.|Two different Factors, not two copies of one.|A password plus a PIN is usually the same knowledge-factor category.
identity|SSO|Single Sign-On|Lets an authenticated user access multiple applications through a common sign-in arrangement.|Sign in Once, open several services.|SSO centralizes a valuable session that needs strong protection.
identity|SAML|Security Assertion Markup Language|Exchanges XML-based security assertions for federated identity.|Security Assertions Move Logins between organizations.|SAML assertions require signature, audience, and time validation.
identity|OIDC|OpenID Connect|Adds an identity layer on top of OAuth 2.0.|Open ID Connects identity to OAuth.|An ID token has a different purpose from an access token.
identity|OAuth|Open Authorization|Delegates limited access to resources using an authorization framework.|Open the door to Authorized access without sharing the password.|OAuth alone is not an authentication protocol.
identity|LDAP|Lightweight Directory Access Protocol|Accesses directory information such as users and groups.|Look up Directory Accounts through a Protocol.|LDAP security depends on authentication and transport configuration.
identity|LDAPS|Lightweight Directory Access Protocol over TLS|Protects directory access with TLS on a dedicated secure connection.|LDAP wears a Secure transport layer.|LDAPS conventionally uses TCP 636; LDAP StartTLS is a different connection setup.
identity|RBAC|Role-Based Access Control|Assigns permissions through roles.|Roles Bring Access to a Crew.|Role assignments still need appropriate scope and least privilege.
identity|ABAC|Attribute-Based Access Control|Evaluates attributes against access policies.|Attributes Bring Access under Conditions.|Attributes may describe subject, resource, action, and environment.
identity|DAC|Discretionary Access Control|Allows an owner or authorized principal to delegate access.|The owner Decides At their discretion who Can access.|DAC differs from centrally enforced mandatory labels.
identity|MAC (access control)|Mandatory Access Control|Enforces access through centrally defined labels and policy.|Mandatory rules outrank the owner's choice.|This MAC means access control, not a network address or cryptographic code.
identity|PAM|Privileged Access Management|Controls and monitors privileged access.|Privileged Accounts need a Manager.|Standing administrator rights should not be granted merely for convenience.
identity|PIM|Privileged Identity Management|Governs privileged role assignment and activation.|Privileged Identities are Managed over time.|In Microsoft Entra, eligibility and active assignment are different states.
identity|JIT|Just-In-Time|Grants a capability or access only when needed.|Just In Time means no permanent open door.|Time limits complement rather than replace authorization checks.
identity|TOTP|Time-Based One-Time Password|Derives short-lived passcodes from a secret and time step.|Time Turns Over the Password.|TOTP codes can still be phished; protect the shared seed.
identity|HOTP|HMAC-Based One-Time Password|Derives one-time passcodes from a secret and counter.|H moves with a counter, while TOTP moves with time.|Counter synchronization is relevant to HOTP validation.
identity|FIDO2|Fast Identity Online 2|Combines standards for strong public-key authentication.|Fast Identity uses keys Online.|Authenticator, relying-party, and user-verification behavior must be evaluated.
crypto|AES|Advanced Encryption Standard|Provides a standardized symmetric block cipher.|Advanced Encryption uses the Same secret on both sides.|AES key size is different from its 128-bit block size.
crypto|DES|Data Encryption Standard|Names a legacy symmetric block cipher with a small effective key size.|DES is the Dated Encryption Standard cue.|Single DES is obsolete for protecting current sensitive information.
crypto|3DES|Triple Data Encryption Standard|Applies a DES-based construction multiple times.|Three DES operations make Triple DES.|Triple DES is legacy and should be replaced by modern approved choices.
crypto|RSA|Rivest-Shamir-Adleman|Provides a public-key algorithm family used with suitable encryption or signature schemes.|Remember the three surnames: Rivest, Shamir, Adleman.|Secure padding, key sizes, and implementation matter.
crypto|ECC (cryptography)|Elliptic Curve Cryptography|Builds public-key systems using elliptic-curve mathematics.|Elliptic Curves carry Cryptographic keys.|This ECC differs from error-correcting memory codes.
crypto|ECDH|Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman|Establishes shared key material using elliptic curves.|Elliptic Curves help two sides agree on a Hidden secret.|Key agreement needs authentication to resist an active intermediary.
crypto|ECDSA|Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm|Creates and verifies digital signatures using elliptic curves.|Elliptic Curves Digitally Sign Assertions.|Signature verification provides no message confidentiality.
crypto|SHA|Secure Hash Algorithm|Names standardized cryptographic hash algorithm families.|Secure Hash makes a fixed-size fingerprint.|A hash is not reversible encryption; algorithm version matters.
crypto|SHA-256|Secure Hash Algorithm with 256-bit output|Produces a 256-bit digest in the SHA-2 family.|256 is the fingerprint length in bits.|A plain hash does not authenticate a sender.
crypto|MD5|Message Digest 5|Produces a legacy 128-bit digest.|Message Digest five is a historical fingerprint.|MD5 collision weaknesses make it unsuitable for security signatures.
crypto|HMAC|Hash-Based Message Authentication Code|Authenticates messages using a secret key and a hash construction.|Hash plus a secret Makes an Authentication Code.|Both parties holding the same secret can create valid tags.
crypto|MAC (cryptography)|Message Authentication Code|Provides a keyed integrity and authenticity tag.|Message Authenticity Checked using a secret.|This MAC is neither a network address nor an access-control model.
crypto|PKI|Public Key Infrastructure|Manages certificates, trust, and public-key lifecycle processes.|Public Keys need an Infrastructure of trust.|A certificate is trustworthy only within a validated trust model.
crypto|CA|Certificate Authority|Issues and signs digital certificates under a policy.|Certificate Authority signs the identity card.|A trusted CA does not guarantee safe behavior by the certificate holder.
crypto|CSR|Certificate Signing Request|Requests a certificate for a public key and associated information.|Certificate wanted: Send Request.|A CSR contains the public key; keep the private key private.
crypto|CRL|Certificate Revocation List|Publishes certificates revoked by an issuer.|Certificates Revoked go on a List.|Freshness and availability affect revocation checking.
crypto|OCSP|Online Certificate Status Protocol|Queries certificate revocation status online.|Online Certificate Status is checked through a Protocol.|An OCSP response needs validation and freshness checks.
crypto|TLS|Transport Layer Security|Protects communications through authenticated and encrypted transport sessions.|Transport Locks the Session.|Use modern protocol versions and validate peer identity.
crypto|SSL|Secure Sockets Layer|Names the obsolete predecessor family to TLS.|SSL is the old Secure Socket label.|Products may say SSL informally while actually using TLS.
crypto|HSM|Hardware Security Module|Protects cryptographic keys and performs controlled cryptographic operations.|Hardware Safeguards key Material.|An HSM does not replace key lifecycle policy or application authorization.
defense|CIA|Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability|Frames three fundamental information-security objectives.|Keep secrets, keep accuracy, keep access.|Here CIA is a security triad, not an intelligence agency.
defense|DLP|Data Loss Prevention|Detects or controls inappropriate movement of sensitive data.|Data Leaving gets Protection.|DLP decisions depend on classification and policy quality.
defense|IDS|Intrusion Detection System|Detects suspicious or policy-violating activity.|Intrusion Detected: Sound the alarm.|An IDS is usually focused on detection rather than inline blocking.
defense|IPS|Intrusion Prevention System|Detects and can block suspicious traffic inline.|Intrusion Prevented before it passes.|Blocking rules can also disrupt legitimate traffic.
defense|NIDS|Network Intrusion Detection System|Analyzes network activity for intrusion indicators.|Network traffic feeds the Intrusion Detector.|Encryption and visibility boundaries limit what it can inspect.
defense|HIDS|Host Intrusion Detection System|Monitors a host for suspicious activity or changes.|Host events feed the Intrusion Detector.|Host telemetry complements rather than replaces network visibility.
defense|SIEM|Security Information and Event Management|Collects and correlates security events for analysis.|Security Information and Events Meet in one place.|A SIEM needs useful telemetry, detections, and operating processes.
defense|SOAR|Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response|Coordinates security tools and response workflows.|Security teams SOAR through automated response steps.|Automated actions need carefully scoped permissions and safeguards.
defense|SOC|Security Operations Center|Organizes security monitoring, investigation, and response.|Security Operators Coordinate here.|A SOC is an operating capability, not simply a room of screens.
defense|EDR|Endpoint Detection and Response|Collects endpoint telemetry and supports investigation and response.|Endpoints Detect trouble and Respond.|Coverage depends on sensor health, supported platforms, and configuration.
defense|XDR|Extended Detection and Response|Correlates detection and response across multiple security domains.|eXtend Detection beyond one endpoint.|Product integration and actual telemetry coverage vary.
defense|MDR|Managed Detection and Response|Provides detection and response as a managed service.|Managed defenders Detect and Respond for you.|Responsibilities and response authority depend on the service agreement.
defense|NAC|Network Access Control|Controls device admission or access to networks.|Network Admission gets Checked.|Admission decisions should not rely only on spoofable MAC addresses.
defense|NGFW|Next-Generation Firewall|Adds application-aware inspection and related capabilities to firewalling.|Next Generation looks beyond simple port numbers.|The label does not guarantee that every inspection feature is enabled.
defense|WAF|Web Application Firewall|Filters HTTP application traffic using security rules.|Web Applications get a Filter.|A WAF does not fix the underlying vulnerable code.
defense|CASB|Cloud Access Security Broker|Applies visibility and control to cloud-service use.|Cloud Access has a Security Broker.|Deployment modes affect visibility and enforcement coverage.
defense|CSPM|Cloud Security Posture Management|Finds cloud configuration and posture issues.|Cloud Settings need Posture Monitoring.|Posture findings still require ownership, prioritization, and remediation.
defense|CVE|Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures|Assigns identifiers to publicly disclosed vulnerabilities.|Common Vulnerability gets an Entry number.|A CVE identifier alone does not communicate severity or exploitability.
defense|CVSS|Common Vulnerability Scoring System|Scores technical vulnerability severity using defined metrics.|Common Vulnerabilities receive a Severity Score.|CVSS severity is not the same as organization-specific business risk.
defense|KEV|Known Exploited Vulnerabilities|Identifies vulnerabilities known to have been exploited in the wild in CISA's catalog.|Known attackers Exploit these Vulnerabilities.|Absence from the catalog does not mean a vulnerability is safe.
attack|DoS|Denial of Service|Disrupts legitimate access to a service.|Deny others their Service.|An availability outage is not always malicious DoS.
attack|DDoS|Distributed Denial of Service|Disrupts service using multiple distributed sources.|Distributed attackers Deny the Service together.|Mitigation often requires upstream capacity and filtering.
attack|MITM|Man-in-the-Middle|Interposes an attacker between communicating parties.|Someone is In The Middle of the conversation.|Also called an on-path attack; authenticated encryption helps resist it.
attack|AiTM|Adversary-in-the-Middle|Interposes a malicious service in an authentication or communication flow.|Adversary Intercepts The Meeting.|Some attacks proxy sign-in and capture sessions even when MFA is used.
attack|XSS|Cross-Site Scripting|Injects script into a trusted web execution context.|Cross-site Scripts Sneak into a page.|Output encoding must match context; CSP is an additional defense.
attack|CSRF|Cross-Site Request Forgery|Tricks a browser into sending an unwanted authenticated request.|Cross-site Requests are Forged using the browser's session.|Anti-CSRF protection differs from output encoding used against XSS.
attack|SQLi|Structured Query Language Injection|Alters database queries through unsafe handling of untrusted input.|SQL input Invades the query structure.|Use parameterized queries; input filtering alone is insufficient.
attack|SSRF|Server-Side Request Forgery|Makes a server issue attacker-influenced requests.|Server Sends a Request for the Forger.|Protect internal endpoints and control outbound destinations.
attack|RCE|Remote Code Execution|Allows an attacker to execute code on a remote system.|Remote attackers Command Execution.|Impact depends on the process privileges and accessible resources.
attack|LFI|Local File Inclusion|Includes unintended local files through an application flaw.|Local Files are Included where they should not be.|File inclusion and arbitrary file read are related but distinct behaviors.
attack|RFI|Remote File Inclusion|Includes attacker-controlled remote resources through an application flaw.|Remote Files Invade the application.|Prevent unsafe dynamic inclusion and constrain resource loading.
attack|APT|Advanced Persistent Threat|Describes capable adversaries or campaigns with sustained objectives.|Advanced attackers Persist Toward a goal.|Persistence alone does not establish a particular threat actor's identity.
attack|IOC|Indicator of Compromise|Provides an observable clue that may indicate compromise.|Indicator Offers a Clue.|An indicator requires context and can produce false positives.
attack|IOA|Indicator of Attack|Highlights behavior associated with an attack in progress.|Indicator Of an Action underway.|Behavioral evidence can complement static compromise indicators.
attack|TTP|Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures|Describes adversary goals, methods, and detailed practices.|Tactic is why; Technique is how; Procedure is the specific recipe.|An observed technique alone does not prove attacker attribution.
attack|OSINT|Open-Source Intelligence|Derives intelligence from publicly available information.|Open Sources Inform the investigator.|Public availability does not guarantee accuracy or permission for every use.
attack|OWASP|Open Worldwide Application Security Project|Provides community application-security guidance and projects.|Open Worldwide Application Security is the Project.|OWASP lists are guidance, not exhaustive vulnerability inventories.
attack|SAST|Static Application Security Testing|Analyzes source or related code artifacts without running the application.|Static means inspect the code while it stands still.|SAST cannot observe every runtime configuration or behavior.
attack|DAST|Dynamic Application Security Testing|Tests a running application from its exposed interfaces.|Dynamic means exercise the app while it runs.|DAST coverage depends on reachable functionality and test configuration.
attack|SCA|Software Composition Analysis|Identifies risks in third-party software dependencies.|Software Components get Analyzed.|Dependency inventory alone does not prove a vulnerable path is reachable.
risk|GRC|Governance, Risk, and Compliance|Aligns oversight, risk decisions, and obligations.|Govern the plan, weigh Risk, Check obligations.|Compliance with a requirement does not guarantee security.
risk|BIA|Business Impact Analysis|Evaluates the business consequences of disruption.|Business Interruptions get Analyzed.|BIA informs recovery priorities rather than prescribing a single technology.
risk|BCP|Business Continuity Plan|Plans how essential business activities continue during disruption.|Business Continues according to a Plan.|Continuity includes people and processes beyond IT recovery.

risk|RPO|Recovery Point Objective|Sets the acceptable age of recoverable data after disruption.|Recovery Point is how far back the data may go.|Backup frequency alone does not prove recoverability.
risk|MTTR|Mean Time to Repair|Measures average time to repair a failed item in the defined measurement context.|Mean Time To Repair: average fixing duration.|Some organizations use restore, recover, or resolve; define the metric explicitly.
risk|MTBF|Mean Time Between Failures|Measures average operating time between failures of repairable systems.|Mean Time Between Failures: average working gap.|An average is not a prediction of the next individual failure.
risk|SLE|Single Loss Expectancy|Estimates loss from one event as asset value times exposure factor.|Single Loss is one event's bill.|Inputs are estimates and should have stated assumptions.
risk|ALE|Annualized Loss Expectancy|Estimates annual loss as SLE times annualized rate of occurrence.|Annual Loss adds the expected year's events.|ALE is an estimate, not a guaranteed yearly loss.
risk|ARO|Annualized Rate of Occurrence|Estimates how often an event occurs per year.|Annual Rate counts Occurrences.|A rate can be fractional when an event is expected less than once a year.
risk|EF|Exposure Factor|Estimates the fraction of asset value lost in one event.|Exposure Factor is the fraction exposed to loss.|Express a percentage as a fraction when calculating SLE.
risk|PII|Personally Identifiable Information|Identifies or can be linked to an individual in a given context.|Personal Information Identifies someone.|Legal definitions and handling obligations vary by jurisdiction and context.
risk|PHI|Protected Health Information|Names identifiable health information protected in a HIPAA context.|Protected Health information gets special handling.|Not every health-related datum in every context is HIPAA-covered PHI.
risk|PCI DSS|Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard|Defines security requirements for scoped payment-account environments.|Payment Cards need Data Security Standards.|PCI DSS is an industry standard, not a general privacy law.
risk|GDPR|General Data Protection Regulation|Sets data-protection requirements within its legal scope.|General Data gets Protection through Regulation.|Applicability and obligations depend on the processing and parties involved.
risk|NIST|National Institute of Standards and Technology|Publishes standards, guidance, and technical research.|National standards connect Science and Technology.|A NIST publication is not automatically a legal mandate for every organization.
risk|RMF|Risk Management Framework|Structures security and privacy risk management across a system lifecycle.|Risk is Managed through a Framework.|Risk acceptance belongs to authorized decision-makers, not a scanning tool.
risk|SDLC|Software Development Life Cycle|Organizes software planning, development, operation, and retirement.|Software Develops through a Life Cycle.|Security activities belong throughout the lifecycle.
risk|SBOM|Software Bill of Materials|Inventories software components and their relationships.|Software ingredients go on a Bill Of Materials.|An SBOM describes components; it does not itself prove they are secure.
