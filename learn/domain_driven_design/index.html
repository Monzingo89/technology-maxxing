Domain-Driven Design

modeling complex business domains in software

2026 · Daily Agent

Why learn Domain-Driven Design?

Domain-Driven Design helps teams handle complex business logic by aligning software models with real business language and boundaries. It reduces misunderstandings between engineers and domain experts, improves changeability, and keeps critical rules explicit in code.

Where it came from

2003: Eric Evans publishes Domain-Driven Design (the 'Blue Book'). Mid-2000s: Strategic patterns (bounded contexts, context maps) gain wider adoption. 2009-2013: CQRS and event sourcing are often combined with DDD in complex systems. 2013: Vaughn Vernon publishes Implementing Domain-Driven Design, expanding tactical guidance. Late 2010s+: DDD becomes a common fit for backend microservice decomposition.

Build your understanding

Work through these exercises in order. Try the task before opening a reference.

  1. Choose a domain (e.g., payments). Write 10 core terms, one precise definition each, and 3 ambiguous terms to avoid. Validate with a non-engineer.
  2. For an online store, split into 3-5 bounded contexts (catalog, pricing, orders, fulfillment, etc.). Add one upstream/downstream relationship and one anti-corruption layer point.
  3. Implement an Order aggregate with rules: cannot confirm empty order, cannot ship unless confirmed, emit domain events on confirm/ship. Add unit tests for each rule.
  4. Given two teams with conflicting definitions of 'Customer', propose a context map and integration strategy to prevent model leakage.
  5. Take a CRUD-style service and move at least 3 business rules from application service into entities/value objects while preserving test coverage.

Catalog research notes

  • DDD is now commonly paired with microservices via bounded-context-aligned service boundaries.
  • Remote event storming (Miro/Mural/FigJam) has become standard for distributed teams.
  • DDD is frequently implemented with Hexagonal/Clean Architecture in backend systems.
  • Teams increasingly use AI drafts for domain glossaries, then validate with domain experts.
